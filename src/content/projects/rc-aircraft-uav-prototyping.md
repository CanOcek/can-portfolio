## What This Shows

This project started as a practical making loop: sketch an airframe, cut and assemble foam pieces, wire the electronics, test the result outdoors, then repair or redesign whatever the flight revealed. The footage currently included here is intentionally sparse and archival. It shows earlier foam-plane work, not the final two planes, and not the later Fusion 360 or 3D-printing phase.

The value of the project is less in a polished final aircraft and more in the accumulation of engineering instincts. Every version forced tradeoffs between weight, stiffness, center of gravity, control authority, motor placement, and how quickly a field repair could get the prototype flying again.

## Design And Test Loop

The early builds used simple materials because iteration speed mattered. Foam and styrofoam made it possible to cut new surfaces quickly, change proportions, tape over damage, and learn from imperfect flights without turning each test into a high-stakes event.

The recurring questions were practical:

- How much structure is needed before weight starts hurting the design?
- Where should the battery and electronics sit so the aircraft balances correctly?
- Which surfaces need reinforcement after hard landings?
- What can be changed quickly between field tests without rebuilding the entire plane?

That loop later pointed toward a more deliberate delta-wing UAV concept in Fusion 360, where the same aircraft questions could be explored with cleaner geometry and 3D-printable parts.
